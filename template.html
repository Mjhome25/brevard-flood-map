Refresh. Road data is pulled about every 15 minutes while the feed on the office computer is running; NWS alerts refresh live every 5 minutes, and the page reloads itself when newer road data is posted. A red note under the map appears when road data is more than 30 minutes old.

Tiers. FL511 (FDOT), Florida Highway Patrol live CAD and NWS items come from agency feeds. NWS Melbourne storm reports are preliminary: flooding reported to the Weather Service by police, emergency managers, spotters, media or the public (each pin names who). They show green as "NWS storm report", always linked to the NWS report; only reports from a government agency (police, emergency managers, NWS staff, public officials) get the check, naming what that agency reported and "Government verified". When one is at the same intersection as another pin it joins that pin instead of adding a second one. Their map point is approximate. Items marked "unconfirmed" are hand-entered from police, county or news posts; each shows its post time and link. A hand-entered report stays on the map, with the time it was last reported, until an official source says the road has reopened. FL511 items show while that feed lists them; when FL511 drops one it stays on the map, marked no longer listed. An FHP crash (never a road closure, water closure, roadblock or debris) that the FHP page stops listing (gone on two checks in a row) is taken off the active list for now and shown under "No longer listed by FHP" for 24 hours, with a link to FHP. FHP only stops listing an incident; it never says the road is clear, and neither does this map. Road closures, roadblocks, water closures and debris that FHP stops listing stay on the map until a cited source says the road is open.
